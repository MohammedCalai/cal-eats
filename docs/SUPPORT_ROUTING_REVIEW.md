# Support routing and user review — 28 October 2026

The hosted audit found two business-created Cal-Eats requests, one open and one resolved. Both were already stored for the platform team, but neither contained a saved reply. The business portal omitted the initial message from its conversation, the administrator home had no support queue, and transactional mail admitted only customer recipients. These combined to make a business request look like a conversation with itself and left administrators without email alerts.

Business-created requests now always belong to Cal-Eats support. An optional validated restaurant context is stored separately from the restaurant responsible for a customer's order-help request. Business requests cannot appear in a restaurant's incoming customer queue or be read by another restaurant. The administrator dashboard shows the bounded list and count of platform requests waiting for a team reply, with links directly to their conversations. Original requests, replies, recipient names and waiting states are visible in both the tablet/browser portal and Cal-Eats Admin source. Native business users can contact Cal-Eats separately from handling customer requests. Earlier replies can be loaded, and case-specific component keys prevent drafts from carrying into another request.

Support request alerts, requester receipts, replies in either direction and status updates use the existing durable service-email worker for current authorised participants. Original message bodies and sensitive subjects are not copied into email. Email links open the administrator, business or customer conversation appropriate to the recipient; business sign-in preserves a permitted internal return path. Existing messages and outbox dispatch states are preserved by migration 29. Existing open business requests receive missing in-app administrator notifications, without a bulk send of historical email. Recipient identity, account state, membership, message source, role and email mode are checked again immediately before dispatch. Uncertain delivery is not automatically resent.

Focused checks cover the complete business-to-admin exchange, duplicate submission/reply recovery, role and restaurant isolation, original-message and paged-reply retention, email links and private content, disabled recipients, customer order-help routing and migration preservation. The related customer journey checks cover ordering, owner-controlled delivery fees, order messaging in both directions, CRM replies and offer redemption. Both native platforms bundle, and the business app passes TypeScript. These checks complement user-interface walkthroughs; they are not a claim of zero defects or signed-device/store readiness.

The local browser walkthrough additionally verified administrator and business email-style links through signed-out login, a business follow-up re-entering the administrator dashboard, customer help receipt/resolution/reopening, customer service chat appearing in the correct restaurant inbox, selection and delivery checkout of a free-item offer, the gift in the saved order, order confirmation with ETA, preparation/ready/out-for-delivery actions and a restaurant order-message reply. It also exposed a predefined collection-only response on a delivery order. Predefined shortcuts now match the order's fulfilment; restaurant-written custom responses remain intact.

Local checks use synthetic accounts and local email files. Hosted email acceptance and the deployment revision must be checked separately after deployment. A provider-accepted message is not independent proof that it reached the recipient's inbox. Physical iPhone/Android, background push and the previously deferred provider/store verifications remain separate release work.
